# Additional references for the acute outcome extension

Verified on 17 November 2026. These references support interpretation of the additional nutrition and hydration analyses and the limits of administrative-complexity adjustment. They do not supply or replace any InEK outcome counts.

| Reference | DOI and PMID | Supported interpretation | Limits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Schisterman EF, Cole SR, Platt RW. Overadjustment bias and unnecessary adjustment in epidemiologic studies. Epidemiology. 2009;20:488-495. | DOI:10.1097/EDE.0b013e3181a819a1; PMID:19525685 | Adjustment for intermediates or their downstream proxies can distort a total causal association. | Applied to PCCL as a methodological concern; this paper does not establish the temporal ordering of diagnoses in InEK. |
| FOOD Trial Collaboration. Poor nutritional status on admission predicts poor outcomes after stroke: observational data from the FOOD trial. Stroke. 2003;34:1450-1456. | DOI:10.1161/01.STR.0000074037.49197.8C; PMID:12750536 | Baseline undernutrition was associated with six-month death after adjustment including stroke severity and prestroke function. Nutritional vulnerability may predate swallowing impairment. | Observational baseline analysis of trial participants, distinct from the randomized FOOD feeding-policy results. Does not establish a dysphagia-caused nutritional deficit. |
| Crary MA, Humphrey JL, Carnaby-Mann G, et al. Dysphagia, nutrition, and hydration in ischemic stroke patients at admission and discharge from acute care. Dysphagia. 2013;28:69-76. | DOI:10.1007/s00455-012-9414-0; PMID:22684924 | A small acute-stroke cohort linked dysphagia with impaired hydration but not worse nutritional status over the first week. | Small cohort; biochemical and clinical measures differ from German diagnosis coding. |
| Bond VE, Doeltgen S, Kleinig T, Murray J. Dysphagia-related acute stroke complications: A retrospective observational cohort study. J Stroke Cerebrovasc Dis. 2023;32:107123. | DOI:10.1016/j.jstrokecerebrovasdis.2023.107123; PMID:37058873 | Australian administrative records showed an association of dysphagia coding with dehydration after adjustment using available variables. | Admission NIHSS and onset timing were unavailable; code definitions differ from German E86, and the study does not validate the German codes or establish full severity adjustment. |
