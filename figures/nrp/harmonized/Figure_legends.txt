Fig. 1 Recorded clinical outcomes across dysphagia-code groups. Points show the percentage of all-age acute-care episodes with each recorded outcome or diagnosis: 33,643 with and 245,634 without secondary dysphagia coding. Circles denote coded dysphagia (D); open squares denote its code-absent comparator. Whiskers are 95% Wilson intervals and can be narrower than the symbols. Numerical columns show percentages. The J12-J18 pneumonia row is a directly queried unique-episode union; the combined pulmonary union uses exact inclusion-exclusion with the observed J69.0 overlap. Pulmonary rows overlap and are not additive. The broad malnutrition row counts each episode with E40, E41, E42, E43, E44.0, E44.1 or E46 once. It overlaps with the individual malnutrition rows. U69.01 and U69.04 are admission-relative pneumonia-timing markers, can overlap and do not establish timing relative to dysphagia. The diagnoses are coded associations, not verified incident or causal complications

Fig. 2 Standardized risk ratios comparing episodes with and without dysphagia coding. Circles and the numerical column show the age–diagnosis model for 14 endpoints in 279,255 adults aged 18–74 or ≥75. Diagnosis groups were I63, I61 and all other principal diagnoses. Squares and triangles show separate models adding sex (279,248 episodes) or patient clinical complexity level (PCCL, 279,254 episodes) for seven endpoints. Whiskers show nominal 95% confidence intervals. PCCL can incorporate complications during admission, and sparse strata limit interval precision. Procedure estimates describe recorded care. FEES, flexible endoscopic evaluation of swallowing. PEG, percutaneous endoscopic gastrostomy.

Fig. 3 Hospital stay and recorded swallowing-related care. (a) Whole-hospital length-of-stay differences, coded dysphagia minus no coded dysphagia, with nominal 95% intervals. The unadjusted adult comparison and age–diagnosis standardization use 279,255 episodes; the separate discharged-alive standardization uses 266,506 adults. Within-DRG estimates concern all-age episodes and use observed dysphagia-group DRG weights, covering 98.28% of dysphagia episodes overall and 95.05% among those discharged alive. The dotted separator distinguishes these reference populations and estimands; all contrasts are descriptive rather than avoidable bed-days. (b) Recorded FEES, simple endotracheal intubation and PEG with the thread-pull technique in the all-age cohort. Circles denote coded dysphagia (D; n = 33,643), open squares its comparator (n = 245,634), and whiskers 95% Wilson intervals. Each numerator counts episodes with the selected code, not procedure repetitions. The PEG code does not cover all gastrostomy techniques; FEES coding does not establish clinical indication, unmet need or all instrumental swallowing examinations. DRG, diagnosis-related group; FEES, flexible endoscopic evaluation of swallowing; PEG, percutaneous endoscopic gastrostomy

Fig. S1 Mortality associations under separate simultaneous standardizations in the OPS-defined acute stroke-care cohort. Adults aged 18 years or older. Filled circles show directly standardized risk ratios comparing episodes with a secondary R13.0, R13.1 or R13.9 code with episodes without these codes. Open squares show the crude risk ratio recalculated on exactly the same common-support population as its paired standardized estimate; the displayed N is the total number of retained episodes. Whiskers are approximate 95% confidence intervals. Each model uses fixed pooled weights within its retained strata. Age comprises 18–74 and 75+ years; principal diagnosis comprises I63, I61 and all other principal diagnoses. Models containing sex and models containing PCCL are separate; no simultaneous sex-by-PCCL adjustment is implied. PCCL models are descriptive sensitivities and do not establish adjustment for baseline neurological severity. Different models can retain different episodes and reference distributions; differences between their estimates are not causal effects or causal mediation estimates. In the age–diagnosis–PCCL model, boundary-risk strata contribute zero plug-in variance and may make intervals too optimistic; see Table S3
